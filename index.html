Research Title
Keyword Extractor

An academic skill that identifies meaningful keywords and multi-word phrases from research paper titles, classifies them, and explains their relevance while preserving the title's original meaning.

Suman Kumar25MA0014Department of Humanities and Social SciencesIIT (ISM) Dhanbad

Abstract

Research titles often contain important concepts that help readers understand a study's subject and scope. This project presents a Research Title Keyword Extractor skill designed to identify important words and meaningful phrases from an academic title. It accepts a title, preprocesses the text, separates individual words and phrases, extracts subject-specific terms, and organises the results into main keywords, supporting keywords, research concepts, and population or context keywords when applicable. Slash commands allow the user to choose operations such as extraction, phrase identification, simple or academic presentation, classification, counting, and explanation. The skill is designed to preserve the original meaning and avoid unrelated or unsupported keywords. Its main limitation is that extraction is based on the title supplied: it cannot represent the full contents of a paper without access to the paper itself.

Objective

  • Identify the most meaningful words and phrases in a research title.
  • Separate important academic terms from common grammatical words.
  • Classify extracted terms by their role and relevance.
  • Provide optional explanations and a count of unique keywords.
  • Preserve the meaning of the original title and avoid inventing unrelated concepts.
  • Offer a simple command interface that makes the skill easy to use.

Research Question

How can an LLM-based skill identify, organise, and explain meaningful keywords and key phrases from academic research titles while preserving their original meaning?

Supporting questions

  • How can important academic terms be distinguished from common grammatical words?
  • How can extracted terms be grouped into useful categories?
  • How can command switches provide different views of the same title?

Methodology

The skill follows a structured title-processing workflow. The stages below are taken from the supplied skill specification.

  1. Input module: Receive a research paper title and check whether a meaningful title has been provided.
  2. Text preprocessing: Remove unnecessary punctuation, identify common grammatical words, and preserve academic terminology.
  3. Tokenisation: Divide the title into individual words and identify meaningful combinations of words.
  4. Keyword extraction: Identify the main research topic, subject-specific terms, and any population, context, or research area stated in the title.
  5. Keyword classification: Organise terms into main keywords, supporting keywords, research concepts, and population/context keywords where applicable.
  6. Output: Present the result in a structured table or list, adding explanations or a keyword count when requested.
Implementation note: The interactive example on this webpage is a small browser-based demonstration using simple text rules. It illustrates the workflow; it is not a live connection to an LLM or a complete implementation of the uploaded skill.

Command Interface

Commands are written at the beginning of a prompt. Compatible switches can be combined. /simple and /academic conflict and require the user to choose one.

CommandPurpose
/extractExtract important keywords.
/phrasesIdentify meaningful multi-word phrases.
/simplePresent keywords in simple language.
/academicPresent keywords in academic language.
/classifyOrganise keywords into categories.
/countDisplay the total number of unique extracted keywords.
/explainExplain the relevance of each keyword.
/resetRestore default settings.

Limitations

  • Title-only analysis: The skill analyses the supplied title and does not automatically read the full research paper.
  • Context limitation: A short or broad title may not provide enough information to identify a population or research setting.
  • Meaning can be ambiguous: A word may have different meanings in different academic disciplines; the title alone may not resolve the ambiguity.
  • Language scope: The supplied specification sets English as the default language. It does not establish validated multilingual performance.
  • Output is not a full literature analysis: Extracted terms should not be treated as a summary of the paper's findings or arguments.
  • Demo scope: The interactive webpage uses basic browser-side rules and may not match the judgement of a full LLM skill.

Future Work

  • Evaluate extraction quality on a collection of research titles from different academic disciplines.
  • Extend and test support for additional languages, including Indic languages.
  • Improve recognition of discipline-specific terminology and multi-word expressions.
  • Compare the extracted keywords with author-provided keywords and expert judgement.
  • Develop an integration that connects the webpage interface to the actual LLM skill.
  • Add export options for saving results in a research-friendly format.

Conclusion

The Research Title Keyword Extractor provides a structured approach to identifying and organising important terms from academic research titles. Its command-based design supports extraction, phrase identification, classification, counting, and explanation. By prioritising meaning preservation and avoiding unrelated terms, the skill can assist early-stage academic reading and topic identification. However, its output must be interpreted within the limits of title-only analysis and should not replace reading the full research paper.
